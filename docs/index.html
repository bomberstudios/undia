The notebook

undia is a half notebook, half philosophy experiment: one pen, one page, and one day to fill it. At midnight, yesterday becomes read-only and you get a fresh blank page.

minifesto

less is more.
more is boring.

undia is a happy idea I had while thinking about constraints in software design.

Most notebooks give you everything: infinite pages, layers, undo, a hundred brushes… I wanted something as close to a scratch paper as possible.

So undia gives you one blank page a day and one writing tool: the digital equivalent of a Uni PIN Fine Line 0.2 (best pen ever for note taking, in my humble opinion).

The rest is an exercise in restraint: everything the app lacks is the result of me spending a lot of time thinking about how to reduce features, visuals, or interactions.

  • No undobecause time only flows forward
  • No eraserbecause all things leave some trace
  • No settingsbecause life’s too short
  • No socialsbecause inside › outside

How it works

Four ideas:

1

One page per day

undia opens (fast!) on today’s page, ready to write (or draw, or make silly sketches).

2

Renews at midnight

At 00:00, undia automatically saves your page and gives you a new blank one.

3

The past is read-only

Of course you can revisit the past. You can even copy it, save it as a PDF, or share it. But you can’t change it.

4

Empty days give you a koan

Skipped a day? No problem! You get a very bad joke short koan to meditate about.

The pen draws. Fingers do everything else.

Do this To get this
Draw with the pen A 0.2 mm black line.
Tap with one finger

Switch between pen and highlighter.

The mark at the top right shows which is on: a smallfor the pen, a largerfor the highlighter.

The highlighter is a 4 mm yellow stroke (light gray on e-ink) that keeps your ink readable underneath. It switches back to the pen by itself 0.8 seconds after a stroke, because why would you have to remember to switch back, in the year 2026 of our Lord?

Swipe left or right Next or previous day. You can’t go past today (both literally and figuratively).
Double-tap Back to today. Very useful when you’ve spent the last 45 minutes reading very bad koans from the past year.
Hold a finger down Open the menu: calendar, copy, save as PDF, share, about.

Under the hood

Plain files, zero lock-in.

Plain text, open format

One SVG file per day, named yyyy-MM-dd.svg. Open them in any browser or vector editor. They’re your notes, not mine.

Easy to share

Copy a page as a PNG, save it as a vector PDF, or share it with Android’s share sheet (LocalSend works great).

How it was made

Half me, half robot.

I’ve been making software for a long time. But I have zero experience with Android app development, and learning Kotlin felt insane a bit overkill for this small project.

So I paired with a large language model (LLM) to write the code for me.

I do what I do best: think about the user experience, the design, and the idiotic jokes you’ve read on this page. The LLM does what it does best: write code.

I tweaked lots of little things, and tested all of it on my own Boox tablet, manually, like an animal. So, any bugs you find are probably my fault 😅.
