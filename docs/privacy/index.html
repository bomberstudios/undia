undia

Privacy policy

undia collects nothing.

Short version: your notes stay on your device, undia can’t connect to the internet, and nobody (me included) sees what you draw.

The longer version follows, because the stores want one.

This policy covers the undia app for Android, and the undia website. Last updated 22 October 2026.

What undia stores

Each day’s page is saved as a small SVG file in the app’s private storage on your device, and nowhere else. There are no accounts, no sign-in and no cloud. If you uninstall the app, the pages go with it.

What undia collects and shares

Nothing. The app doesn’t ask for the internet permission, so it has no way to send anything off your device. It has no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no third-party services that receive your data. It doesn’t ask for your location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files.

When something leaves the app

Only when you ask it to. “Copy to clipboard” puts an image of the page on your clipboard. “Save as PDF” writes a file to your Downloads folder (or the app’s Download folder on older versions of Android). “Share…” opens Android’s share sheet, and the app you pick receives the PDF. After that, what happens to the file depends on that app, not on undia.

This website

The undia website (undia.naranjanaranja.org) counts visits with a privacy-friendly, self-hosted analytics tool that doesn’t use cookies for tracking (Umami).

The website doesn’t collect your notes, because it has no idea they exist. The app and the website are separate and don’t communicate in any way.

Children

undia is safe for children: it doesn’t collect, sell, or track anything. It is non-toxic, non-poisonous, and non-irritant, and it may only harm kids if they are offended by any of the bad puns the app shows on days with no notes.

Changes

If this ever changes, for example if a future version adds something that talks to the internet, I’ll update this page and the date at the top before that version ships.
